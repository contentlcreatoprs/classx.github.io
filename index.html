How to Study in Class 10 and Excel in Studies

A practical guide to studying smarter, understanding concepts, preparing for exams and building habits that can help you perform at your best in Class 10.

L
Lakshya Pratap Singh September 23, 2026 · 8 min read

Class 10 is an important academic year. For many students, it is the first time they feel serious pressure about board examinations. But studying successfully is not simply about sitting with books for many hours.

The real goal should be to understand what you study, revise it regularly, practise questions and learn how to manage your time effectively.

“Don't study only for marks. Study to understand, practise and improve. Good marks are a result of good preparation.”

1. Understand the Syllabus First

Before starting serious preparation, know exactly what you have to study. Go through your syllabus and divide each subject into chapters and topics.

This prevents you from studying randomly and helps you understand how much of the syllabus is still left.

Study Tip Make a simple checklist of every chapter. Tick a chapter only after you have studied, practised and revised it.

2. Make NCERT Your Foundation

For CBSE Class 10 students, NCERT textbooks should form the foundation of preparation. Read the chapter carefully instead of simply memorising answers.

Pay special attention to definitions, examples, diagrams, formulas, activities and questions given in the textbook.

3. Study Every Day Instead of Studying Everything at Once

Consistency is more useful than studying for extremely long hours occasionally. A student who studies regularly can gradually build strong understanding and reduce exam-time pressure.

A simple daily structure

  1. Revise something you studied previously.
  2. Learn a new concept.
  3. Practise questions based on that concept.
  4. Review mistakes before finishing your study session.

4. Focus on Understanding, Not Memorising Everything

Memorisation has its place, but understanding concepts makes learning much easier. Ask yourself why a formula works, how a process happens and how a concept can be applied to a different question.

For example, in Mathematics, don't only memorise a formula. Understand where and when it should be used.

5. Practise Questions Regularly

Reading a chapter can give you the feeling that you know everything. Solving questions tells you whether you can actually use what you learned.

After completing a topic, practise questions of different levels of difficulty.

  • NCERT examples and exercises
  • Previous-year questions
  • Sample papers
  • Competency-based questions
  • Questions you previously got wrong

6. Keep a Mistake Notebook

One of the most useful study habits is maintaining a small notebook for mistakes.

Whenever you make an important mistake in Mathematics, Science, Social Science or another subject, write down the question and understand why your answer was incorrect.

Why it works Your mistakes show you exactly where you need improvement. Revising them regularly can prevent you from repeating the same errors in exams.

7. Use Active Recall

Instead of repeatedly reading the same page, close your book and try to explain what you remember.

You can ask yourself questions such as:

  • What were the main points of this chapter?
  • Can I explain this concept without looking at the book?
  • What formulas do I remember?
  • Can I solve a question based on this topic?

8. Revise at the Right Time

Revision should not be left entirely for the days before the examination. Short and regular revision sessions can help you remember what you have learned.

A simple approach is to revise a topic after learning it, then revisit it later while continuing with new chapters.

9. Learn How to Write Answers

Knowing the answer is only part of exam preparation. You also need to communicate it clearly on the answer sheet.

  • Read the question carefully.
  • Answer according to what is actually asked.
  • Use appropriate headings where useful.
  • Show important steps in numerical questions.
  • Use diagrams when they are relevant.
  • Keep your presentation neat and readable.

10. Practise Full-Length Sample Papers

Once you have completed a substantial part of the syllabus, start solving papers under exam-like conditions.

Set a timer, avoid looking at notes and attempt the paper as if you were sitting in the actual examination.

After finishing, check your answers and analyse where you lost marks. The analysis is just as important as solving the paper.

11. Avoid These Common Study Mistakes

  • Studying only one or two days before an examination.
  • Watching study videos without actually practising.
  • Memorising answers without understanding concepts.
  • Ignoring difficult chapters for too long.
  • Solving questions without checking mistakes.
  • Spending too much time making notes instead of learning.
  • Studying continuously without taking reasonable breaks.

12. Take Care of Your Sleep and Routine

Good preparation is not only about books. Sleep, regular breaks, physical activity and a balanced routine can help you stay focused and avoid unnecessary exhaustion.

Avoid making extremely long study schedules that are impossible to maintain. A realistic routine that you follow consistently is more useful than a perfect timetable that you abandon after a few days.

Final Thoughts

Excelling in Class 10 does not require studying every minute of the day. It requires consistency, understanding, practice and regular revision.

Start with your textbook, understand each concept, practise questions, learn from your mistakes and gradually increase the difficulty of your preparation.

Most importantly, don't compare your progress constantly with other students. Focus on improving your own preparation every day.

“Small improvements every day can create a big difference by the time your examinations arrive.”
